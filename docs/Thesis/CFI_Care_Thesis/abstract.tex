\chapter*{Abstract}
% \addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\label{chap:abstract}
Healthcare systems today are characterized by fragmented patient records distributed across disconnected institutions, often inaccessible to patients and clinicians. In Egypt, this fragmentation is especially severe due to paper-based workflows in public facilities and isolated digital systems, resulting in disrupted continuity of care and incomplete clinical visibility. CFI-Care is motivated by the need to address this structural gap in healthcare information continuity.
This thesis addresses fragmented healthcare information systems in which patient records are distributed across multiple providers, leading to reduced continuity of care. The scope of this work is the design and development of CFI-Care (Collaborative Flow of Intelligence for Care), a patient-centric platform for unifying distributed medical data into a secure and interoperable longitudinal health record system.
The system is built on an HL7 FHIR-compliant interoperability layer that standardises clinical data into structured healthcare resources. It introduces a graph-based medical history model representing patient records as a temporally evolving directed acyclic graph, enabling branching and reconstruction of clinical reasoning pathways over time.

A dual-view interface provides categorical and temporal representations of patient history, allowing clinicians to explore relationships between medical events and maintain multiple diagnostic hypotheses. A mobile OCR pipeline digitises legacy medical documents into structured FHIR resources.

The mobile application extends the platform with IoT and wearable integration via Health Connect, image quality assurance using Laplacian blur detection and enhancement pipelines, real-time push notifications via cloud messaging, and caregiver proxy sessions enabling secure delegated access through controlled authentication handshakes.

Clinical decision support is provided through a Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework that grounds outputs in verified patient data. Access control is enforced via a consent-driven, role-based security model ensuring auditable data sharing.
These results demonstrate the feasibility of integrating graph-based patient modeling, interoperable healthcare standards, and grounded clinical AI within a unified secure platform, with potential generalization to similar fragmented healthcare environments.
