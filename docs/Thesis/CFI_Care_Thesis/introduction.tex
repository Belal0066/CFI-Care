\section{The Global Digital Health Revolution}
\hspace*{2em}Healthcare is shifting from reactive treatment toward proactive, data-driven prevention. 
The global digital health market was valued at roughly USD 288 billion in 2024 
and is projected to approach USD 946 billion by 2030, growing at more than 22\% annually. 
This acceleration is powered by mobile health, telemedicine, and Artificial Intelligence (AI), 
which promise cheaper, faster, and more effective care. 
Yet adoption is uneven: while leading systems deploy AI for personalized care, 
many \ac{LMICs} still struggle with basic record keeping because of weak infrastructure, unclear policy, and fragmented data.

\section{The Situation in Egypt}
\hspace*{2em}Egypt, home to over 106 million people and the largest healthcare market in MENA, 
has improved outcomes but faces a persistent data void. 
Records in many Public Health Centers (PHCs) remain paper-based, locked in filing rooms, and rarely interoperable across facilities. 
Digital systems that do exist often operate in silos, so a patient moving between providers loses continuity of care. 
Data quality is frequently incomplete or error-prone, and patients seldom provide formal written consent, reflecting limited trust and awareness of data value. 
This fragmentation prevents AI from learning accurate patterns and delivering benefits to Egyptian patients.

\section{Problem Statement}
When a patient sees multiple specialists, their medical records scatter across
disconnected systems. Radiology images sit at one hospital, lab results at
another, prescriptions at a third. Clinicians piece together incomplete
histories from memory or phone calls. Patients have no way to track their own
health trends over time. Care coordination breaks down because nobody has the
full picture. Egypt needs modern, AI-enabled healthcare but lacks the
foundational data ecosystem. The core challenges are:
\begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Data Fragmentation:} Records are scattered across non-interoperable environments with no single longitudinal home.
      \item \textbf{Analog Dominance:} Paper-first practices impede automated text parsing, 
      secure remote sharing -patients cannot easily access or share their own histories- and historic pattern analysis. %longitudinal tracking and lossless sharing.
      % \item \textbf{Lack of Control:} Patients cannot easily access or share their own histories.
      \item \textbf{Absence of Clinical Data Visualizations:} Traditional systems lack an interconnected mapping of complex patient histories, 
      forcing clinicians to make decisions using incomplete timelines.
      \item \textbf{Privacy and Consent Gaps:} The lack of standardized, auditable consent flows that safeguard data distribution.
      \item \textbf{Missed AI Opportunities:} Absent or inaccessible digital data blocks safe clinical decision support 
      such as Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).
\end{itemize}

\section{The Proposed Solution: CFI-Care (Collaborative Flow of Intelligence for Care)}
\hspace*{2em}To resolve these structural vulnerabilities, 
this work presents \textit{CFI Care}: an AI-powered, collaborative, patient-centric health record platform 
that unifies diverse medical streams into a single secure ecosystem while keeping patients in control.
Unlike conventional EHR models, \textit{CFI Care} re-engineers data ingestion, 
session representation, and access management through an integrated multi-tier paradigm:
\begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Unified Records:} Consolidates data from disparate sources into a single, 
      secure repository with timeline visualization.
      \item \textbf{Medical History Graphs:} Replaces flat tables with an interactive, 
      multidimensional graph (inspired by distributed version control systems such as git), 
      which allows clinicians to trace complex diagnostic branching paths, 
      concurrent chronic disease developments, and subsequent treatment plan merges. 
      This gives medical teams a holistic overview of clinical data  %Tracks evolving treatment plans as a medical history graph to reveal branches, merges, and longitudinal trends.
      \item \textbf{Dynamic Access Control framework:} Implements a dynamic authorization middleware protocol and \ac{RBAC}, 
      empowering patients to issue time-bound, auditable consent to data access grants to specific practitioners or caregivers, tracking all state transitions in a secure pipeline.
      \item \textbf{AI Integration (RAG):} Grounds generative summaries and answers in the patient's own records to support safer diagnostics and triage.
      \item \textbf{Mobile Digitization:} A camera scanner digitizes legacy paper reports, crowdsourcing data ingestion despite PHC device constraints.
\end{itemize}

\section{System Integration and Prototype Realization}
\hspace*{2em}A core contribution of this thesis is the concrete development, 
engineering, and validation of a fully operational end-to-end \textit{CFI Care} platform prototype 
realized through a modern, distributed architecture composed of:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A responsive web application built with \textbf{Angular}, providing clinicians with complex graphical rendering capabilities via D3.js timelines and sandboxed iframe operations.
    \item A native mobile application developed using \textbf{Flutter}, allowing patients and caregivers to manage identity tokens, view cross-device notifications, interact with Android Health Connect streams, and execute paper-to-FHIR document parsing.
    \item A secure, centralized server suite driving an asynchronous, event-driven \textbf{Node.js Backend-for-Frontend (BFF)} middleware pipeline, backed by an industrial-grade \textbf{Keycloak} Identity Provider, a \textbf{Redis} high-speed transaction cache layer, and a verified \textbf{HAPI FHIR} resource server infrastructure.
\end{enumerate}


\section{Thesis Objectives}
This thesis aims to design, implement, and validate the CFI Care platform to modernize health data
management in Egypt.

The specific engineering and research milestones comprise:

\begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Isolating Systemic Barriers:} Documenting the structural challenges within Egypt's healthcare sector, specifically regarding
            infrastructure gaps and consent practices.
      \item \textbf{Engineering a multi-tiered system topology:} Designing and compiling a secure, scalable, and usable architecture for patient-centric
            records and clinician workflows.
      \item \textbf{Enforcing Zero-Trust Access Mechanics:} Implementing robust RBAC and \ac{JIT} consent middleware framework capable of safeguarding private patient data while
            allowing them to delegate temporary access to it when needed.
      \item \textbf{Constructing Grounded Medical Intelligence Services:} Engineering AI (RAG) services that can interpret medical records and deliver
            actionable insights.
      \item \textbf{Empowering Edge Ingestion and Ownership:} Implementing computer-vision and layout-analysis pipelines on portable devices and empowering patients with tools for data ownership, appointments, caregiver
            sharing, and mobile digitization.
\end{itemize}
